**Make fewer decisions—especially fewer important ones.** Every decision consumes energy, so build general decision frameworks to preserve your attention for what truly matters. Warren Buffett, for example, categorizes most investments into three buckets: invest, don’t invest, and don’t understand. That last bucket alone saves him enormous time and effort.
**Find and amplify your unique leverage. ** One useful way to think about this is to ask: without you, what critical decisions would not get made? Those decisions define your unique impact.
**Stop doing things you would never start.**  An ever-expanding to-do list without a strong stop-doing list is a sign of weak discipline. The core question is: if you were starting today, would you still choose to do what you’re currently doing? If not, what exactly are you holding on to?
**Run lean. Stay small.**  As organizations grow, internal matters tend to crowd out external impact, shifting attention toward management rather than contribution. Hire better people, let them focus on truly important work, and expand their responsibilities. Fewer people, smaller scale, and less internal activity all mean lower management overhead.
**Be useful. ** Don’t obsess over how to succeed—that’s an outcome. Focus instead on how to be useful to others. Usefulness is the cause; success is merely the result.
